GEDSI & Stakeholder Engagement in Climate Mitigation

Bring meaningful, legally grounded stakeholder voice into your mitigation planning — practical guidance for engaging communities and embedding Gender Equality, Disability and Social Inclusion (GEDSI) considerations, as required under the Climate Change Act.

Why Stakeholder Engagement Matters

The Climate Change Act requires local governments to engage stakeholders meaningfully in climate change planning. Genuine engagement ensures that mitigation strategies are legitimate, effective, and equitable. It also builds trust and social license for climate interventions that may require changes to community life.

Understanding GEDSI

GEDSI stands for Gender Equality, Disability and Social Inclusion. It is a legal requirement under South African law (the Constitution, the Gender Equality Act, the Persons with Disabilities Act and others) that must be integrated into all government planning and implementation.

In the context of climate mitigation, GEDSI means ensuring that:

  • Women and girls have equal access to climate benefits and are not disproportionately burdened.
  • People with disabilities have equal access to climate information, services and decision-making.
  • Marginalised communities — including informal settlements, rural populations and youth — are meaningfully included in climate planning.

Practical Steps for Inclusive Engagement

Effective stakeholder engagement and GEDSI integration requires deliberate planning:

  1. Map stakeholders — Identify all relevant groups, including those who are typically excluded.
  2. Choose inclusive methods — Use accessible venues, formats, and languages. Provide childcare, transport support, and disability accommodations.
  3. Ensure follow-through — Document how stakeholder input shaped the final plan and report back to participants.
  4. Monitor & evaluate — Track whether mitigation actions are delivering equitable outcomes.

Who Is This Guide For?

This module is designed for those responsible for stakeholder engagement and inclusive planning — including municipal climate officers, community development workers, NGO practitioners, and civil society organisations working on climate action.
